# Line Editing

Line editing improves prose, clarity of narrative, and effective communication one paragraph or similarly bounded passage at a time. It is neither exhaustive critique nor copy correction.

## Read in context

Before revising a unit, determine:

- what job it performs in the agreed scope;
- what the reader knows at its opening;
- what claim or transition the next unit needs from it;
- which factual claims, qualifications, citations, and technical terms must remain.

Check relevant sources when accuracy is part of the scope. Do not turn source checking into a separate review pipeline.

## Paragraph loop

For the current paragraph or passage:

1. Briefly identify the main communication problem when a diagnosis will help the author evaluate the draft.
2. Present one complete proposed revision. Include the original alongside it when the author requests comparison or when the changes would otherwise be hard to assess.
3. Explain only substantive choices, factual corrections, or meaningful tradeoffs. Let the draft carry minor edits.
4. Wait for the author's response. Their returned wording becomes the current draft.
5. Revise at the magnitude requested until the author accepts the passage, then follow the agreed application protocol before moving on.

When the author disagrees, first recover the function they are protecting. Offer a narrower revision that preserves it instead of defending the earlier draft. Explicit author preferences outrank generic stylistic heuristics unless they create a factual error or serious ambiguity; explain that conflict directly when it occurs.

## Boundaries

- Preserve quantitative details, evidentiary limits, citations, and intentional framing.
- Do not silently solve a structural problem with connective prose. Flag the structural issue and let the author decide whether to switch levels.
- Do not inventory every possible flaw. Focus on the changes that improve the current unit.
- Do not rewrite merely to make the prose sound like the agent. Match the surrounding voice and the author's accepted drafts from the current session.
- Do not save chat drafts to working files. Apply only settled prose when the working agreement calls for source edits.
